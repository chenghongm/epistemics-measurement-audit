# Readout-format diagnostic results — 2026-11-13

This is a small, targeted pilot, not the final new-dialogue study. Two models read the same four saved Llama histories: two topics, each at opening and one pressure turn. For each history, two option orders and two discount settings were crossed with the three formats described in `format_control_plan.md`, for 48 branches per model. The Qwen runs read Llama-generated conversation histories, not Qwen's own generated dialogues. Local weight revision is unknown. Selection was purposive and follows the earlier v2 smoke findings. No statistical inference or general prevalence claim is justified.

All 96 branches completed; 32/32 letter probes had probability mass at least 0.9 (minimum 0.9982). All 64 prose outputs ended with EOS. Raw output and exact prompts: `results/format_control/llama.json` and `results/format_control/qwen.json`. Automated prose side was checked against the explicit option wording for all 64 outputs; one response contains a conflicting displayed letter and must remain ambiguous when treating its whole answer as a verdict.

The run outputs are explicitly included in Git despite the general `results/` ignore rule. ## Results

- Llama labelled prose (B) printed `(B)` in all 16 branches and followed the text in the second slot. In every one of 8 pairs, swapping option order switched the option selected by the text. The labelled probe (A) did not cross the 0.5 threshold on swapping for any of these 8 pairs; this does not mean the probabilities were identical or the probe is unbiased.
- Llama unlabelled prose (C) changed its chosen side across option orders in 4/8 pairs. It nonetheless generated `(B)` in 6/16 answers even though neither option was labelled in its readout prompt; past conversation still contained letters. It switched sides between B and C in 4/16 same-history, same-order, same-discount pairs. Output format is causally manipulated here, but this comparison alone cannot identify which component of the label/punctuation change or prior conversation caused it.
- Qwen labelled prose switched chosen side across option orders in 1/8 pairs, and unlabelled prose in 0/8. B and C text selected different options in 1/16 matched comparisons. One further labelled output (four-day week, pressure, discount, order 1) states `(A) Keep the five-day week`, but the prompt's A was `Move to a four-day week`: its letter and words conflict. Counting that as a decisive stance is inappropriate. Therefore the 1/16 difference refers to the explicit wording, with one other pair ambiguous under full-answer review; the order comparison for labelled Qwen similarly has one ambiguous pair. All Qwen unlabelled prose answers had no explicit option letter.
- For both models the 16 individual labelled probes on each model were mass-qualified, but high mass indicates letter-compliance, not valid stance measurement. One Qwen value rounded to 1.00000003 due to floating-point accumulation; do not interpret as above one in exact arithmetic.

Example of a paired difference: on Llama's four-day-week opening, without discount and in order 1, labelled prose chose `Keep the five-day week` while unlabelled prose chose `Move to a four-day week`. Different answers under a controlled prompt alteration show this specific readout depends on how options are presented; they do not establish the model's internal stance.

Qwen sometimes adopted erroneous numerical descriptions from the saved plastics history. Flag factual errors separately: arithmetic misinterpretation, user-provided hypothetical evidence, and changes in output format cannot be conflated.

## Practical implication

Neither presentation is validated for the main experiment. Retain both option orders and raw wording, treat letter/text conflict as ambiguous, and report position sensitivity. Run the same controls on fresh conversations generated by each model and include more topics before asserting generality. Full matching between letter and prose requires labelled options for both; show the unlabelled prose as a sensitivity condition, not as a fully matched substitute. The experiment does not establish whether opening letters cause the behaviour, nor whether the displayed-label effect is larger than all possible anchoring effects.
